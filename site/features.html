Features

Everything FramePlayerX does. The user guide explains how to use each part.

Cinema

Watch flat videos in a full-size cinema instead of on a floating screen: the Classic, a stadium hall with red velvet seats, or the Luxe, an open room with neon lights and leather recliners. Pick your row and how much the screen curves around you, and the house lights dim as the film starts and come up when you pause.

Playback

Video formats

180°, 360°, fisheye, flat and interlaced (1080i, DVD) videos, in 2D or 3D: side by side or top/bottom at full or half size, frame sequential, and 3D Blu-ray (disc images, MKV rips and .ssif files) in full 3D. The format is detected automatically, and you can change it for any video.

Hardware decoding

HEVC (H.265) in 8 and 10 bits and H.264, up to 8K, play on the Frame's own video decoder, even past a damaged stretch of a file; AV1, VP9, interlaced video and anything else play with software decoding.

Performance

Out of the box, 8K 60 fps video, 10-bit included, plays at full frame rate with the display at 120 Hz and 3744 pixels per eye, and the CPU stays almost idle: the decoder's pictures are drawn without being copied, and foveated rendering that follows your eyes keeps full detail where you look. Refresh rate, resolution and foveation can be changed in Settings, even while a video plays.

HDR

HDR10 and HLG videos are fitted to the Frame's screen scene by scene, and colours it can't show are toned down instead of cut off.

Passthrough videos

See your room around the people in passthrough videos, and behind green or blue screens.

Subtitles and spatial audio

Subtitles from a file, inside the video or found on OpenSubtitles from the player and kept on your headset with their timing set for each video, picked in your language and shown in any script, and 360° audio that follows your head.

Your videos

Videos on the headset
Browse any folder on the headset, a microSD card or a USB drive, including cards formatted on Windows or a Mac, and make libraries of your folders, with thumbnails, filters and sorting.
Media servers
Browse and play from Jellyfin, Emby and Plex, which can transcode while you watch, sync watch state and show their preview pictures above the seek bar; any server with a DeoVR feed or HereSphere API, transcoding when it offers that, syncing watch state with HereSphere and showing timeline previews with DeoVR; and network shares (SMB, NFS), SFTP servers, WebDAV, DLNA servers and web folders.
Watch history, Up next and playlists
Carry on where you left off, queue videos to play one after another, a folder's subfolders included, in order or shuffled, and loop the queue or a single video; keep playlists of videos from any source, mark favorites and ratings, and delete the files of videos you're done with.
PIN locks
Hide a source behind a PIN until you unlock it.

Comfort and control

Adjustments
Position, zoom, depth, comfort focus to keep what you look at whole as you tilt your head or lie down and ease its depth up close, picture settings and fisheye lens calibration, kept for each video or saved as presets to use on any video, and a screen that follows your head for watching lying down.
Custom controls
Choose what each controller button does, or go hands-free: point with your head, and rest its dot on a button to press it.
Phone remote
Control the player from your phone's browser, which pairs with a code the headset shows and can type into the headset too, or from apps made for DeoVR's remote.
Haptic devices
Optional, off until turned on; syncs supported haptic devices with videos that have scripts.

And the rest

Languages

English, German, Spanish, French, Japanese and Simplified Chinese, following the language Steam is set to; the phone remote follows the headset. Help translate.

Updates

Checks for a new version when it starts, and installs it from Settings when you choose to, on a Stable or a Beta channel.

No FramePlayerX account or tracking

It connects to the servers you add, to Plex's account services if you link Plex (to sign in, find your servers and renew access), to OpenSubtitles when you use Find subtitles or sign in to it (the video's title, year, episode and IDs, and a fingerprint of the file) and to GitHub to check for updates, and sends nothing after a crash unless you choose to send the report, which you can read first (no video names, folders or server addresses).

Install FramePlayerX

It's free. Install it on the headset in Desktop Mode, with no PC needed, or from a PC.

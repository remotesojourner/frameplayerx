Features

Everything FramePlayerX does. The user guide explains how to use each part.

Cinema

Watch flat videos in a full-size cinema instead of on a floating screen: the Classic, a stadium hall with red velvet seats, or the Luxe, an open room with neon lights and leather recliners. Pick your row and how much the screen curves around you, and the house lights dim as the film starts, as far and as slowly as you like, and come up when you pause.

Playback

Video formats

Flat, 180°, 360° and fisheye videos, in 2D or 3D: side by side or top and bottom at full or half size, or frame sequential, and interlaced video from TV recordings and DVDs. The format comes from the file's metadata, its name or its shape, and you can change it for any video.

3D Blu-rays

Disc images, MKV rips and .ssif files play in full 3D, each eye at full 1080p. A disc image plays its main title, with its chapters and its audio and subtitle languages.

Fisheye lenses

Presets for 180°, 190°, 200° and 220° lenses and the angles you add, with sliders for a lens's edges and centre for footage straight from a camera.

Hardware decoding

HEVC in 8 and 10 bits, H.264, and AV1 on SteamOS 0.5.10 and later play up to 8K on the Frame's own video decoder, even past a damaged stretch of a file. Anything else plays with software decoding, and the control bar says why.

Performance

Out of the box, 8K 60 fps video, 10-bit included, plays at full frame rate with the display at 120 Hz and 3744 pixels per eye, and the CPU stays almost idle. Foveated rendering follows your eyes, keeping full detail where you look; refresh rate, resolution and foveation can be changed in Settings, even while a video plays.

HDR

HDR10 and HLG videos are fitted to the Frame's screen scene by scene: midtones keep their brightness, highlights keep their detail, and colours the screen can't show are toned down instead of cut off.

Seek bar previews

Pointing along the seek bar shows a picture of that moment: the server's own previews where it makes them, else one taken from the video.

Chapters and bookmarks

A video's chapters and your own bookmarks, each a press away; grip and X or Y step through them.

Clock

A clock above the controls shows the time and when the video will end, and pressing the video's length shows how much is left. A 24-hour clock is a switch in Settings.

Picture and comfort

Adjustments

Position, size, depth and eye alignment for each video, with keyframes that change them smoothly along it.

Presets

Save adjustments as a preset to give any video at once, and step through presets with a button while you watch.

Picture

Brightness, contrast, saturation, gamma and sharpening for each video, or as the start for every video.

Comfort focus

Keeps what you look at whole as you tilt your head or lie down, and eases its depth up close, in stereo 180° and fisheye videos.

Watching lying down

Recenter as you lie back, or let a flat screen and the menus follow your head as you turn, lie back or roll onto your side.

Flat screen

Set a flat screen's distance, width and curve and the colour around it, let screen glow light that colour with the picture's edges, and crop a film's black bars off the screen.

Passthrough videos

Videos with their own mask (_alpha) show your room around the people in them, with nothing to set.

Green and blue screens

Remove a green or blue screen, or any colour, to show your room instead. The screen's colour is found in the video, and sliders set how much goes and take its tint off edges.

Your room around the screen

The Room button shows your room around a flat video's screen and the menus.

Subtitles and sound

Subtitles

From inside the video, from files beside it or from your server, in any script the headset has fonts for. They start in your language when the audio is in another, always, or never, as you choose.

OpenSubtitles

Find subtitles from the player by the video's IDs or file name, download them with or without an account, keep them on the headset and shift their timing for each video.

Subtitles in 3D

Set how deep, big and high subtitles show; picture subtitles from Blu-rays are drawn for each eye.

Audio

Pick the audio track and subtitles side by side while you watch, kept with the video, and hear 360° audio that follows your head.

Where your videos come from

On the headset

Any folder on the headset, a microSD card or a USB drive, including cards formatted on Windows or a Mac.

Libraries

Make libraries of your folders, with thumbnails, search, filters and sorting.

Network shares

SMB shares from Windows, Samba and most NAS, and NFS exports.

SFTP, WebDAV and web folders

Any computer you can sign in to with SSH, Nextcloud and other WebDAV servers, and a web server's folder listing.

DLNA servers

Browse and play from DLNA servers on your network.

Jellyfin and Emby

Browse your libraries with filters, and play with the server's transcoding, its watch state and its preview pictures above the seek bar.

Plex

Link your Plex account to find your servers, then browse and play with Plex's transcoding, watch state and preview thumbnails.

DeoVR feeds and HereSphere APIs

Any server with a DeoVR feed or HereSphere API, transcoding when it offers that, syncing watch state with HereSphere and showing timeline previews with DeoVR.

Downloads

Keep videos from shares and servers on the headset, a card or a USB drive to watch with no connection; a download that stops carries on where it got to.

Your library

Home
Resume what you were watching, then Continue watching, your favorites and your sources.
Watch history
Everything you've played, from any source, with search and filters, or no history kept on the headset at all.
Up next
Queue videos to play one after another, a folder's subfolders included, in order or shuffled, and loop the queue or a single video.
Playlists
Keep playlists of videos from any source.
Favorites and ratings
Mark favorites and rate videos, and filter and sort by them.
Deleting files
Delete the file of a video you're done with, on the headset, a share or a server that lets you.
PIN locks
Hide a source behind a PIN until you unlock it.

Control

Custom controls
Choose what each button and thumbstick direction does, with and without the grip held.
Hands-free
Put the controllers down: point with your head, and rest its dot on a button to press it.
Moving things
Drag the screen, the control bar and the menus where you want them, and put them all back with one press.
Phone remote
Control the player from your phone's browser, which pairs with a code the headset shows and can type into the headset too, or from apps made for DeoVR's remote.
Haptic devices
Optional and off until turned on: syncs Intiface Central, TCode devices and The Handy with videos that have scripts.

And the rest

Languages

English, German, Spanish, French, Japanese and Simplified Chinese, following the language Steam is set to; the phone remote follows the headset. Help translate.

Updates

Checks for a new version when it starts, installs it from Settings when you choose to, on a Stable or a Beta channel, and then shows what's new since the version you had.

Send diagnostics

Report a problem from Settings › About: you can read the report first, and you get a reference to quote when you tell us about it.

No FramePlayerX account or tracking

It connects to the servers you add, to Plex's account services if you link Plex (to sign in, find your servers and renew access), to OpenSubtitles when you use Find subtitles or sign in to it (the video's title, year, episode and IDs, and a fingerprint of the file) and to FramePlayerX's website, or GitHub when the website doesn't answer, to check for updates, and sends nothing after a crash, or when you report a problem, unless you choose to send the report (no video names, folders or server addresses).

Install FramePlayerX

It's free. Install it on the headset in Desktop Mode, with no PC needed, or from a PC.
